Surgery

Nose surgery

The nose sits in the middle of the face, so a change in shape is easy to see. The clinic treats rhinoplasty as a common first operation for people entering cosmetic surgery, and also describes it for breathing problems, injury and differences present from birth.

What it treats

  • Tell the doctor about medical history, previous surgery and allergies, including aspirin and Brufen.
  • Avoid vitamin C, alcohol and pain-relief medicine.
  • Sleep for at least eight hours.
  • You can go home the same day.
  • Keep your head raised, with extra pillows or a recliner, to limit bleeding and swelling. Do this on the first night.
  • Leave packing and splints in place. If packing blocks the nose, breathe through the mouth. Do not pull packing or splints out.
  • Some bleeding for two or three days is described as expected. Call the doctor if bleeding seems heavy.
  • Do not blow the nose for at least a week. Avoid heavy lifting and hard exercise.
  • If you sneeze, try to do it with your mouth open.
  • Do not take aspirin. It slows clotting.
  • Stitches come out at 7 to 14 days.

The process

    The plan

    Once someone decides to change the shape, the doctor explains which shape may suit that nose. It is not the same plan for every face. The clinic says it uses silicone imported from the United States, chosen for a natural-looking line, and that the doctor carves the implant to the individual nose.
